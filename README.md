# UshmaLens

**Ward-level heat-health early warning for Indian cities**

*Forecasting not what the weather will be, but what it will do to people, ward by ward, five days ahead.*

Hourly heat physiology → Human Thermal Stress Index with ensemble probabilities → excess deaths per ward → Heat Action Plan levels → CAP 1.2 alerts and SMS/WhatsApp advisories in 7 languages.

4 cities · 417 real municipal wards · Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Chennai, Hyderabad

</div>

## Where this came from

UshmaLens merges two projects built for the same problem statement (extreme-heat early warning with a Human Thermal Stress Index), keeping the stronger half of each:

| | **USHMA** (Odisha) | **TAPAS / ThermaLens** (4 cities) | **UshmaLens** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wards | 332, synthetic boundaries | **417 real wards** with satellite, LCZ, MODIS, Census HL-14, OSM | TAPAS's real wards and data |
| Physics | **Hourly Liljegren WBGT, human-Tmrt UTCI, Heat Index; 37 reference tests** | UTCI with an *estimated* radiant temperature | USHMA's physics, on hourly NWP inputs including measured-model radiation |
| Live weather | Open-Meteo script | OpenWeatherMap, radiation estimated from cloud cover | Open-Meteo NWP **plus the 31-member GFS ensemble** |
| Index | 4-channel HTSI, WBGT-only anomaly (designed for humid Odisha) | Multiplicative H×V×E×(1−AC) | 4-channel HTSI, made **regime-aware** (humid *and* dry heat), recalibrated on ward-level data |
| Uncertainty | Conformal bands from an Odisha-trained model | Confidence that decays with lead time | **Ensemble probability that each ward crosses each band edge** |
| Mortality | Excess deaths = baseline × (RR−1), transferred curve | Logistic score, published RR anchors | USHMA's excess-death model **anchored per city to TAPAS's published RRs** (de Bont 2024), with ward vulnerability and cooling access as effect modifiers |
| Alerts | **HAP state machine with hysteresis, CAP 1.2, 7 audiences × 3 languages** | Cooldowns, trilingual SMS with state language | USHMA's engine, **7 audiences × 7 languages** (en, hi, mr, gu, ta, te, or) |
| Planning | **Cooling-centre max-cover, counterfactuals** | **Preventive-measure simulator, allocation, CSV audit** | All of them, on one forecast |
| History | 11 years Odisha NASA POWER (not shipped) | None | **12+ years of hourly ERA5 per city: climatology, backtest, replay of any past heatwave** |
| Stack | Python science + Node server + React | Python + vanilla JS | **One Python process** (FastAPI) + React dashboard |

## What the backtest shows

Every number below is regenerated by `scripts/build_history.py` from hourly ERA5 data (2014 to Oct 2026), run through exactly the physics the live forecast uses.

| City | Warning+ days, Mar–Jun | Warning+ days *missed* by the IMD temperature rule | Driven by humid heat | Worst episode replayable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ahmedabad | 3.9% | 67 of 81 | 43% | May 2024, peak HTSI 93.5 |
| Chennai | 2.6% | 65 of 65 | 28% | Jun 2014, peak 85.8 |
| Hyderabad | 2.7% | 41 of 43 | 19% | Apr–May 2024, peak 83.8 |
| Mumbai | 0.1% | 2 of 2 | 100% | May 2024, peak 75.4 |

- **Chennai never meets IMD's coastal heat-wave rule in 12 years of ERA5, yet has 65 Warning-level days.** Humid heat is invisible to a thermometer rule.
- ERA5 under-reads city station maxima by 1–3 °C, so these counts understate IMD heat-wave days. Treat the comparison as indicative.
- Mumbai's low count matches the epidemiology: de Bont et al. (2024) found Mumbai's heat–mortality link the weakest of ten Indian cities.

Two calibration findings shaped the design. Both are documented in the code where the decision lives:

1. **USHMA's anomaly channel was WBGT-only.** Run on Hyderabad, it ranked the lethal May 2015 dry heatwave as ordinary, and the city's worst "episodes" came out as August monsoon days. The anomaly is now judged on whichever regime is more unusual for that place and date: WBGT for humid heat, air temperature for dry heat. The night channel gained a hot-dry-night route as well. The worst episodes now land on the real pre-monsoon heatwaves.
2. **Ward-level urban heat islands lift the index.** USHMA's band edges (calibrated on 27 km Odisha cells) put the median ward at Warning+ on 8–15% of summer days, which is alert fatigue. The edges are now recalibrated by USHMA's own rule (top ~10/3/1% of summer days) on the ward-level distribution: **Watch 61.1 · Warning 72.6 · Emergency 78.3** (`data/build/calibration.json`).

## How it works

```
Open-Meteo NWP (per ~5 km cell) ─┐                       ERA5 2014–2026 (per city)
GFS ensemble, 31 members ────────┼─► hourly Liljegren WBGT · human-Tmrt UTCI · Heat Index
recent ERA5T history ────────────┘        │                          │
                                          ▼                          ▼
                         daily max / night min / hours > 30 °C   day-of-year climatology
                                          │                          │
          ward UHI uplift (satellite built-up + LCZ + MODIS LST) ──► HTSI 0–100
                                          │      intensity · anomaly (humid|dry) · night relief · duration
                                          ▼
               ensemble spread → P(Watch / Warning / Emergency) per ward per day
                                          │
   Census vulnerability × cooling access × district HVI ──► excess deaths, MRI, ED surge
                                          │      baseline × pop × (RR−1), RR anchored to published local estimates
                                          ▼
             Heat Action Plan state machine (forecast-driven, no flapping) per ward and city
                                          │
     CAP 1.2 · SMS / WhatsApp / webhooks · 7 languages × 7 audiences · checklist · audit
                                          │
       React + Leaflet dashboard · cooling-centre optimiser · intervention simulator · ASHA field view
```

The **live** and **replay** modes share one code path. Replay plays any window of the ERA5 record forward day by day. Its forecasts are perfect-prognosis (the observed weather) with lead-time error bands, so you can audit the alerts a past heatwave would have produced. A labelled **drill** (+1 to +4 °C) runs on top of either mode. CAP documents from replay and drill are status `Exercise`, and messages are prefixed `[EXERCISE]`.

When an input fails, the system degrades and labels it, never silently:

- **Deterministic feed rate-limited:** the ensemble control run stands in, at city level.
- **Ensemble down:** uncertainty falls back to lead-time error widths.
- **Both down:** the last good response is reused, marked stale, for up to 30 h.
- **Nothing available:** the system stays in replay.

## Quickstart

Prerequisites: Python 3.10+, Node.js 18+.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

```bash
cd dashboard && npm install && npm run build && cd ..
```

```bash
python -m ushmalens
```

Open **http://127.0.0.1:8000**. It opens on a replay of the May 2024 Ahmedabad heatwave. Press **Play** to watch levels escalate, pick another episode from the clock, or press **Go live** for today's forecast (it is fetched in the background at startup). API docs are at `/docs`.

The ERA5 history and derived artefacts are shipped in `data/`. To rebuild them from scratch:

```bash
python scripts/fetch_era5.py
```

```bash
python scripts/build_history.py
```

Tests (index reference values, HTSI behaviour, risk model, HAP state machine, all 7 languages, CAP validity, API):

```bash
python -m pytest tests -q
```

## Configuration

| Variable | Default | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `USHMALENS_API_KEY` | `ushmalens-dev-key` | Required in `x-api-key` for routes that move the clock, dispatch or change subscriptions |
| `USHMALENS_PORT` / `USHMALENS_HOST` | `8000` / `127.0.0.1` | Server bind |
| `USHMALENS_LIVE_REFRESH_S` | `10800` | Background live refresh interval |
| `USHMALENS_NOSCHED` | off | Disable the background fetch (tests, offline demos) |
| `USHMALENS_CHANNEL_MODE` | `simulated` | Set `twilio` with `TWILIO_ACCOUNT_SID`, `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN`, `TWILIO_SMS_FROM`, `TWILIO_WHATSAPP_FROM` for real SMS/WhatsApp |
| `USHMALENS_WEBHOOK_SECRET` | dev secret | HMAC-SHA256 key for `x-ushmalens-signature` on webhooks |
| `USHMALENS_CAP_SENDER(_NAME)` | demo identity | The issuing authority's identity in CAP |

Model constants (HTSI weights, UHI coefficients, HAP triggers, intervention effect sizes, crude death rates) are in `ushmalens/config.py`. Each one carries the reason it has its value.

## Limitations, up front

- **No death data was fitted.** No paired local weather–mortality series exists for these cities. Curves are anchored to published relative risks; Hyderabad and Chennai use the ten-city pooled estimate. Hospitalisation surge is an assumed multiple of excess deaths, carried as a range.
- **Ward population is modelled.** City Census 2011 totals are scaled for growth and split by area × built-up share. Census ward totals do not map onto these boundaries.
- **Vulnerability is mostly city-level.** Most Census indicators exist only per city. Ward-to-ward differences come from cooling access (satellite, HL-14, OSM) and kutcha housing where HL-14 matched.
- **Replay forecasts are perfect-prognosis.** No archived NWP forecasts were used, so replay shows what the alerting would do given a forecast, not the skill of a specific model. Live uncertainty comes from the GFS ensemble at each city centre, transferred to wards.
- **Bias between datasets.** ERA5 sets the climatology, while live mode uses NWP. A bias between them shifts the anomaly channel.
- **WBGT tail.** Liljegren WBGT on reanalysis inputs reaches 40–41 °C on a handful of days per city (99.9th percentile under 40 °C).
- **Translations are drafts.** Hindi, Odia, Marathi, Gujarati, Tamil and Telugu text awaits native-speaker review. Specialist audiences fall back to English in the four newer languages.
- **Coefficients are judgements.** UHI coefficients, cooling-access modifiers and intervention effect sizes are stated planning judgements, not fitted values.
